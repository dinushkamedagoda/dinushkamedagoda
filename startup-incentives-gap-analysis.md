# Sri Lanka's Startup Incentive Gap: A Reality Check

*A response to Chalinda Abeykoon's post on emerging market startup ecosystems*

---

## The Right Diagnosis

Chalinda's post nails the core problem: **Founders choose jurisdictions based on incentives, not patriotism.**

He lists what emerging market governments *should* offer:
- ✅ Simpler regulations
- ✅ Strong legal protections  
- ✅ Meaningful tax advantages
- ✅ Access to government procurement
- ✅ Grants or incentives tied to local incorporation

But how does Sri Lanka actually score? Let me answer from my lived experience in corporate compliance.

---

## The Reality: A Case Study in Bureaucratic Friction

### 1. **Simpler Regulations** — F

I recently attempted to engage with the Trade Ministry to discuss startup policy frameworks. The approach seemed straightforward: contact the Secretary to the Trade Ministry, Mr. Vimalendraraj.

**What happened:** His secretary said he doesn't have time.

**Why this matters:** This one person oversees **17+ government departments**. And I later discovered he also serves on the BOI (Board of Investment) board. When the decision-maker tasked with improving the ease of doing business doesn't have time to listen to entrepreneurs describing their actual problems, you've identified your bottleneck.

This isn't an anomaly. This is structural.

The regulatory burden isn't just complicated—it's *inaccessible*. You can't simplify what you don't hear about.

### 2. **Strong Legal Protections** — C+

Sri Lanka's legal system has strong fundamentals (common law heritage, established courts), but founders ask: *Is it predictable? Is it fast?* 

A corporate matter that takes 3-4 months to resolve in Singapore takes 18 months in Sri Lanka. That's not a disadvantage—it's a **deal-killer for venture capital.** VCs price in timeline risk. A 15-month delay = a 15-month funding delay.

### 3. **Meaningful Tax Advantages** — D

Sri Lanka offers some incentives (BOI tax holidays, exemptions), but:
- They're **bureaucratic to access** (back to point #1)
- They're **inconsistently applied** (depends on ministry discretion, not clear rules)
- They're **temporary and unpredictable** (can change with government, don't inspire long-term decisions)

Compare this to Singapore's **flat corporate tax rate** (17%), predictable for 20 years, no surprises.

Founders don't want tax breaks—they want **tax predictability**. Big difference.

### 4. **Access to Government Procurement** — D-

Sri Lanka has set-asides for local SMEs in theory. In practice:
- Procurement is fragmented across 17+ departments (guess who oversees them?)
- Each has different requirements, timelines, approval processes
- The process is opaque—no centralized platform, no standard RFP format

For a startup trying to sell to government, navigating this is like playing 17 different games with different rules simultaneously.

### 5. **Grants or Incentives Tied to Local Incorporation** — C-

We have the BOI, the National Science Foundation, some DFID-funded programs. But:
- **Awareness is low** among the startup community
- **Access is unclear** (who qualifies? How do you apply? What's the timeline?)
- **Distribution is spotty** (if your business doesn't fit a specific agenda, you're out of luck)
- **Funding amounts are small** relative to what founders actually need

## Why This Matters (The Incentive Trap)

When a founder evaluates where to incorporate, they run a mental checklist:

**Sri Lanka checklist:**
- Can I talk to someone in government? ❌ (No time)
- Will my corporate compliance be straightforward? ❌ (17 bureaucrats, each with different rules)
- Will my exit be tax-efficient? ❌ (Uncertain)
- Will I get government contracts? ❌ (Opaque process)
- Will I get grants? ❌ (Small, unpredictable amounts)

**Singapore checklist:**
- Can I talk to someone in government? ✅ (Singapore's Economic Development Board is built for founder engagement)
- Will my compliance be straightforward? ✅ (One clear code, consistently applied)
- Will my exit be tax-efficient? ✅ (Predictable corporate tax, no surprises)
- Will I get government contracts? ✅ (Centralized procurement platform)
- Will I get grants? ✅ (Significant, clear-criteria innovation grants)

**The math is obvious.** The founder picks Singapore—not because they love Singapore, but because the friction cost is lower.

---

## The Real Problem (And It's Not What You Think)

This isn't about money. Sri Lanka can afford to offer competitive grants.

**The real problem is accessibility and predictability.** 

You can't build a startup ecosystem on government discretion. Entrepreneurs are rational actors. They need:
1. **Clear rules** (not bureaucratic judgment calls)
2. **Accessible decision-makers** (not oversubscribed officials managing 17 departments)
3. **Predictable timelines** (not 18-month legal processes)
4. **Transparent processes** (not fragmented approaches across ministries)

---

## What Needs to Change

Governments that want to compete don't redesign incentives. They redesign **systems.**

### Quick wins (6-12 months):
- **Create a single startup portal:** One place for all compliance, all permits, all grants. Modeled on Singapore's, not complex.
- **Establish a Founder's Hotline:** A dedicated person (actually accessible) to help navigate government. ROI: thousands of hours of founder friction eliminated.
- **Publish standard timelines:** "Your BRN will be processed in 3 days, not 3 months." Then enforce it.
- **Centralize procurement:** One platform, one standard RFP format, one timeline.

### Medium-term (1-2 years):
- **Reduce ministry count** (Chalinda mentioned this): You don't need 17 if 3 can do the job. Clear mission, clear accountability.
- **Tax code transparency:** Publish the corporate tax treatment for every business type. No surprises = founder confidence.
- **Legal system efficiency:** Fast-track startup litigation to a dedicated judge/track. 6 months, not 18.

### Long-term (2+ years):
- **Shift from discretion to rules:** Every incentive, every tax break, every grant has a clear *formula*, not bureaucratic judgment.
- **Founder feedback loops:** Make government policy *responsive* to founder pain points, not top-down.

---

## The Bottom Line

Sri Lanka has the *legal and financial tools* to compete. What it lacks is the *systems thinking* to deploy them.

Founders aren't choosing Delaware over Colombo because Delaware is wealthier or smarter. They're choosing it because **you can actually run your business there without waiting 18 months to resolve a contract dispute**.

If we want more companies to incorporate locally, we need to make local incorporation the better *business decision*—not the better patriotic decision.

That starts with someone actually having time to listen.
