# LinkedIn Post: Sri Lanka's Startup Incentive Gap

---

I tried calling the Trade Ministry this week to discuss startup policy.

The Secretary doesn't have time.

He oversees **17+ government departments**. And yes, he's also on the BOI board.

When the person responsible for "ease of doing business" doesn't have time to listen to entrepreneurs describing their actual problems, you've found your bottleneck.

---

**Here's why this matters:**

@Chalinda Abeykoon's post nailed it—founders choose jurisdictions based on incentives, not patriotism.

He asked: How would you rate Sri Lanka on:
🔹 Simpler regulations  
🔹 Strong legal protections  
🔹 Meaningful tax advantages  
🔹 Access to government procurement  
🔹 Grants or incentives tied to local incorporation  

My answer, from 5 years in corporate compliance:

**D+ / C- overall.**

Here's the breakdown:

📋 **Simpler Regulations: D**
You can't access them. Literally couldn't get 5 minutes with the decision-maker.

⚖️ **Strong Legal Protections: C+**
Good laws. But contract disputes take 18 months (vs 3 months in Singapore). VCs price that in = deal killer.

💰 **Meaningful Tax Advantages: D**
We have incentives. But they're bureaucratic to access, inconsistently applied, and temporary. Founders want **predictability**, not tax breaks.

🏛️ **Government Procurement: D-**
Theoretically possible. Practically? 17 different departments, 17 different rules, one opaque process.

📊 **Grants/Incentives: C-**
They exist. But low awareness, unclear access, small amounts relative to need.

---

**The real problem isn't money. It's systems.**

When a founder chooses Singapore over Colombo, they're not choosing because Singapore is smarter. They're choosing because:
- Government is *accessible*
- Rules are *clear*
- Timelines are *predictable*
- Processes are *transparent*

Sri Lanka has talented people and capital is flowing in. But **the system is the bottleneck.**

---

**What would actually work:**

🔸 Single startup portal (one place for ALL compliance, permits, grants)  
🔸 Founder hotline (an actual accessible person)  
🔸 Published timelines ("3 days, not 3 months")  
🔸 Centralized procurement (one RFP format, one timeline)  
🔸 Reduce ministry count (do we really need 17?)

These aren't expensive. They're not revolutionary. They're just *systematic*.

---

**Bottom line:**

You don't win founders with patriotism. You win them by making local incorporation the better *business decision.*

That starts with someone actually having time to listen.
